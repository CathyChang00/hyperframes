# Why the sky is blue

Sunlight contains every color. As it enters the atmosphere, air molecules scatter the shorter blue wavelengths far more strongly than the longer red ones — this is Rayleigh scattering, which scales with 1/wavelength^4. Because blue light is redirected across the whole sky, light reaching our eyes from every direction is dominated by blue, so the daytime sky looks blue. At sunset the light travels through more atmosphere, the blue is scattered away, and the remaining reds and oranges reach us.
