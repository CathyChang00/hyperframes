# A short history of the QWERTY keyboard

QWERTY was designed by Christopher Latham Sholes in the 1870s for the Sholes & Glidden typewriter. A common myth says it was made to slow typists down; in reality it separated frequently-paired letters to reduce mechanical jams on the typebars. Remington commercialized it, and as typewriters spread the layout became a de-facto standard. Despite faster alternatives like Dvorak, QWERTY persisted through network effects and now dominates digital keyboards worldwide.
